# White Glove Service Standard (WGSS)

**Document ID:** WGSS-001  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Foundation draft  
**Owner:** Executive Desk / Service Steward

## 1. Purpose

The White Glove Service Standard defines the minimum acceptable level of care, communication, professionalism, preparation, judgment, and accountability for every Nunn Cloud interaction.

White-glove service is not luxury language, excessive accommodation, or performative politeness. It is disciplined care that makes important work easier, clearer, safer, and more dependable for the customer.

## 2. Service Promise

> Every customer will be listened to, treated with dignity, guided with care, informed with clarity, protected by governance, and supported through completion.

## 3. Seven Service Commitments

1. **Listen before recommending.**  
   We seek to understand the person, organization, objective, constraints, and risk before presenting a solution.

2. **Understand before designing.**  
   We confirm the problem and desired outcome rather than building from assumption.

3. **Explain before implementing.**  
   We ensure the customer understands the purpose, implications, authority, and expected result.

4. **Confirm before executing.**  
   We verify scope, approval, identity, authority, and conditions before consequential action.

5. **Document before closing.**  
   We record material decisions, commitments, unresolved matters, and next actions.

6. **Follow through after delivery.**  
   We confirm outcomes, address remaining needs, and do not disappear after handoff.

7. **Remain accountable throughout the partnership.**  
   We own continuity, communication, recovery, and improvement.

## 4. Required Behaviors

Every representative must:

- arrive prepared;
- know the purpose of the interaction;
- use the customer's time responsibly;
- speak plainly and accurately;
- distinguish fact, interpretation, and recommendation;
- avoid unsupported promises;
- respect the customer's decision rights;
- identify uncertainty openly;
- protect confidential information;
- coordinate internal work without burdening the customer;
- close with a summary, owner, and next action;
- complete promised follow-up;
- escalate concerns before they become failures.

## 5. Prohibited Behaviors

Representatives must not:

- pressure a customer into a decision;
- use fear, hype, or artificial urgency;
- imply authority they do not possess;
- promise outcomes that are not controlled;
- blame the customer for internal confusion;
- expose internal disagreement carelessly;
- make the customer repeat established facts unnecessarily;
- disappear when an issue becomes difficult;
- hide a delay, risk, or mistake;
- substitute friendliness for competence;
- treat governance as an obstacle to bypass.

## 6. Interaction Readiness Checklist

Before an interaction:

- What does the customer need from this interaction?
- What history and commitments already exist?
- What decisions may be required?
- Who has authority?
- What evidence or material should be ready?
- What could create confusion or concern?
- What does successful closure look like?

## 7. Interaction Closure Checklist

Before closing:

- Was the customer's need addressed?
- Did we confirm their understanding?
- Were decisions and limitations clear?
- Were unresolved issues named?
- Is the next action assigned?
- Does the customer know what to expect next?
- Was the appropriate evidence recorded?
- Is follow-up scheduled or otherwise controlled?

## 8. Service Levels by Situation

### 8.1 Routine request

Respond clearly, resolve efficiently, document the action when needed, and avoid unnecessary ceremony.

### 8.2 Important decision

Provide context, evidence, options, implications, recommendation, authority path, and a clean record.

### 8.3 Customer concern

Acknowledge quickly, listen fully, avoid defensiveness, provide ownership and a resolution path.

### 8.4 Service failure

Escalate, protect the customer, communicate facts, offer remedy, verify recovery, and document improvement.

### 8.5 High-risk or regulated request

Slow down appropriately, confirm authority and policy, document approvals, explain constraints, and preserve human oversight.

## 9. Professional Presence

Professional presence includes:

- calm and composed communication;
- respect without excessive formality;
- confidence without arrogance;
- warmth without loss of precision;
- patience without indecision;
- responsiveness without haste;
- discretion;
- reliable preparation;
- ownership.

## 10. Continuity Standard

The customer should experience one institution, not a collection of disconnected individuals.

Therefore:

- records must be current;
- handoffs must be complete;
- commitments must survive personnel changes;
- tone and standards must remain consistent;
- a customer must always know who is accountable;
- no customer should have to rebuild context from the beginning.

## 11. Service Recovery Standard

When the standard is missed:

1. recognize the miss;
2. notify the accountable owner;
3. acknowledge it to the customer when appropriate;
4. correct the immediate problem;
5. restore confidence through clear follow-through;
6. capture the cause;
7. improve the process, training, or control;
8. confirm closure.

## 12. Measurement

The WGSS is measured through:

- customer confidence;
- response and follow-through reliability;
- clarity of next steps;
- number of avoidable customer repetitions;
- number of missed commitments;
- issue recovery outcomes;
- evidence completeness;
- handoff quality;
- customer effort;
- consistency across representatives;
- relationship retention and expansion.

## 13. Representative Creed

> I represent an institution whose trust must be earned repeatedly. I will listen carefully, think responsibly, act only within authority, communicate clearly, preserve dignity, provide evidence, and remain accountable until the work is properly complete.
