# Government Deployment Blueprint

## Purpose

Define the governing blueprint for deploying SentinelOS capabilities into government-aligned operating environments without creating separate architectures or bypassing core governance doctrine.

## Scope

This blueprint defines:

- inherited governance foundations,
- deployment specialization boundaries,
- readiness and proof expectations,
- executive and board operating surfaces,
- AI docking and governed control-surface rules.

## Source Doctrine

The Government Deployment Blueprint inherits from authoritative operating doctrine, including:

- the MOB constant,
- valid MOB overlays,
- Executive and Board governance surfaces,
- evidence, audit, and risk controls,
- readiness interpretation surfaces such as the MRI.

## Top-Level Principles

- Inherit from source doctrine; do not replace it.
- Specialize through profiles, overlays, and addenda.
- Preserve governance, authority, evidence, and audit controls.
- Keep executive, board, and operational surfaces synchronized.
- Separate advisory interpretation from execution authority.

## Baseline Inheritance

GBP inherits from the MOB constant and may use overlays, addenda, and review-held current-state records to remain accurate without replacing the underlying source doctrine.

### Inheritance rules

- GBP inherits from the authoritative MOB baseline.
- Overlays and addenda may clarify current state without replacing the baseline.
- Deployment profiles are specializations, not forks.
- Inherited governance, authority, evidence, and audit controls remain mandatory.
- Blueprint evolution occurs through governed inheritance and synchronization.

## Governance Model

GBP preserves:

- approval boundaries,
- authority validation,
- risk evaluation,
- evidence traceability,
- audit and receipt generation,
- review-held synchronization across Board, Executive, and operational surfaces.

## Evidence and Audit Model

All governed actions and readiness claims should remain backed by:

- evidence references,
- receipt or audit lineage,
- freshness indicators,
- proof-lane classification,
- reviewable supporting records.

## Proof Separation

GBP recognizes separate proof lanes. Completion in one proof lane does not imply completion, approval, or authorization in another.

### Required proof lanes

- local proof,
- internal proof,
- public or external proof.

### Proof-separation rules

- Local validation does not imply public readiness.
- Internal verification does not imply external authorization.
- Evidence may inform another lane without satisfying it.
- Executive, Board, and AI-guided surfaces must preserve these distinctions.
- Readiness claims must identify the lane in which proof was established.

## Control Surfaces

GBP recognizes governed control surfaces for executive review and operational routing.

These include:

- the Government Executive Desk,
- governed APIs,
- approved evidence and reporting surfaces,
- bounded AI docking layers.

Control surfaces do not bypass underlying authority or governance.

## Mission Readiness

GBP uses readiness interpretation surfaces, including the MRI, to present current operational posture.

Readiness surfaces must expose:

- readiness state,
- blockers and degraded factors,
- confidence or freshness state,
- next governed action.

## Deployment Profiles

Deployment profiles inherit from the GBP baseline and specialize mission, environment, and reporting posture without discarding inherited controls.

See:

- `docs/GBP/Phase-4-Deployment-Profiles.md`

## Executive and Board Surfaces

GBP requires synchronized executive and board surfaces.

- Executive surfaces provide governed readiness and action routing.
- Board surfaces provide oversight and review.
- Neither surface independently bypasses core authority controls.

## AI Docking and Control Surface

AI interaction must terminate at a governed control surface rather than directly coupling to SentinelOS internals.
